About Varanasi

Varanasi, also known as Kashi or Benares, is one of the world's oldest continually inhabited cities and the spiritual capital of India.
Situated on the banks of the sacred river Ganges in Uttar Pradesh, the city is a mesmerizing blend of life, death, and spiritual liberation.
With a history spanning thousands of years, Varanasi is famous for its vibrant ghats, ancient temples, intricate silk weaving,
and narrow, bustling alleys. Whether you are witnessing the grand evening Ganga Aarti, taking a peaceful sunrise boat ride,
or indulging in local delicacies like malaiyo and kachori, Varanasi offers an intense, deeply moving, and unforgettable experience.

Hidden Spots to Explore (Other than main Ghats)

While Dashashwamedh Ghat and the main temples are legendary, the true essence of Kashi often lies hidden in its quieter corners. Escape the crowds and explore these offbeat locations:

  • Ramnagar Fort: Located on the eastern bank of the Ganges, this crumbling yet majestic 18th-century sandstone fort is the ancestral home of the Maharaja of Banaras. It houses a quirky museum featuring vintage cars, palanquins, and antique weapons.

  • Lolark Kund: A fascinating and ancient stepwell hidden away near Tulsi Ghat. It is one of the oldest sacred sites in Varanasi, dedicated to the Sun God, and is a brilliant architectural marvel plunging deep into the earth.

  • Saray Mohana (Weavers' Village): Step into the villages near Varanasi to see the magic of Banarasi silk sarees being made. You can watch skilled artisans working on traditional handlooms and learn about the generations-old craft.

  • Bharat Kala Bhavan Museum: Located inside the sprawling, peaceful campus of Banaras Hindu University (BHU), this museum holds an incredible collection of miniature paintings, ancient sculptures, and local historical artifacts.
